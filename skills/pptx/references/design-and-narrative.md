# Design and narrative

## Start from the communication job

Identify who must understand or decide something, what the deck should cause them to believe or do, and the central takeaway. Build a coherent beginning, development, and ending rather than a collection of facts.

Give each slide one primary job. Use assertion-style titles when the evidence supports a conclusion. Prefer fewer meaningful points and split dense material instead of shrinking it.

Write audience-facing copy. Remove implementation notes such as “insert chart,” “talk about,” “draft,” and “speaker note.” Keep terminology, dates, units, and grammar consistent.

## Choose a visual language

Use visual guidance in this order:

1. Supplied template, brand system, or reference deck.
2. Explicit user art direction.
3. A model-chosen, restrained visual language suited to the audience and content.

Do not mix an unrelated default theme into a supplied template. Preserve established theme fonts, colors, recurring elements, and slide families unless redesign is requested.

When no style is supplied, favor clear editorial composition: readable type, balanced margins, strong alignment, useful whitespace, and a small purposeful palette. Avoid generic blue-gradient branding, repeated UI cards, pills, navigation bars, dashboard grids, and decorative charts unless the subject genuinely requires them.

Vary slide silhouettes when the story benefits, while keeping typography, spacing, and color coherent.

## Typography and fit

Use the supplied template font when available. Otherwise choose fonts appropriate to the target platform and content language. There is no universally installed Chinese font across macOS and Windows; prioritize readable substitution unless the user supplies a shared font.

For a 16:9 projection deck, ordinary body copy generally needs more space and larger type than a desktop-read report. Treat any numeric size range as a starting point, not a pass/fail rule. Shorten copy or change composition before using unreadably small type.

Leave extra room for Chinese and mixed-language wrapping. Inspect the rendered result rather than assuming text metrics are identical between LibreOffice and Microsoft PowerPoint.

## Images and diagrams

Choose images for meaning, not decoration. Determine the intended crop before placement, preserve aspect ratio, and keep faces, labels, and product UI away from crop edges. Avoid repeating the same non-background image without a narrative reason.

Use native shapes for simple, editable diagrams. Create connectors before nodes so edges stay behind them. For complex or aesthetic illustrations, prepare an SVG or raster asset instead of forcing a dense shape graph into PowerPoint.
